How Much Protein Do Indian Women Over 40 Actually Need?

And how to get enough without giving up the food you love.

By Sushma · Eat Well Live Well

You are in your 40s.

You’ve probably heard it everywhere lately: Eat more protein.

So you start wondering... How much protein do I actually need? Does every meal need to have protein? Do I need protein powder? And if I eat mostly South Indian food, does that mean I have to start eating chicken and eggs all day?

If you’ve had these questions, you’re not alone. This is something I hear from so many women I coach.

And honestly, I don't want you to start counting every gram of protein tomorrow morning. I want you to first look at what is actually on your plate.

Why does protein become more important after 40?

As we move through our 40s and beyond, maintaining muscle becomes increasingly important. Protein is one of the things that helps support your muscles.

But protein isn't some magic weight-loss food. It doesn't mean that suddenly eating more protein will make the weight disappear.

What it can do is help you build more satisfying meals, support muscle alongside strength training, and make it easier to manage hunger.

And this is where I see many women getting stuck. They are constantly thinking: What should I remove? Less rice. Less roti. No sweets. Smaller portions.

Instead, I want you to start asking: What can I add to make this meal more nourishing and satisfying?

So how much protein do you actually need?

There isn't one magic number that every woman over 40 needs. Your protein needs depend on things like your body size, activity, goals, health and strength training.

So rather than giving every woman the same number, I prefer to start somewhere much simpler: Look at each meal.

Breakfast. Lunch. Dinner. And ask yourself: Where is my protein?

Because one of the biggest problems I see isn't necessarily that women don't eat protein. It's that most of their protein is pushed into one meal — usually dinner.

Let's talk about breakfast first

Think about a typical morning. Tea and biscuits. Or two idlis. Or dosa. Or poha.

Nothing wrong with these foods. But if that's the entire breakfast, where is your protein?

You may feel fine initially. And then 10:30 or 11 comes around... You're hungry again.

This is why I don't tell women to stop eating idli or dosa. I tell them: Let's build the meal better.

  • Idli + generous serving of sambar + curd
  • Dosa + sambar + eggs
  • Dosa + sambar + paneer or another protein source
  • Vegetable upma + curd
  • Poha + peanuts or another protein-rich accompaniment
  • Pongal + sambar + curd
  • Toast + eggs or paneer
  • Oats + Greek yogurt or milk/soy milk

You don't need a completely different breakfast. You just need to stop thinking of breakfast as only a source of energy. It should also help keep you nourished and satisfied.

And then comes lunch...

This is another place where I see a very familiar plate. A big serving of rice. Some poriyal. And maybe a little sambar or dal.

Again — rice is not the problem. The question is whether your overall meal has enough protein, vegetables and fibre for you.

So instead of saying: “I shouldn't eat rice.” Think: “What can I add to this rice?”

  • Rice + sambar or dal + vegetables + curd
  • Rice + fish + vegetables
  • Rice + paneer/tofu + vegetables
  • Rice + sambar + vegetables + another good protein source
  • Chapati + dal/chana + vegetables + curd
  • Chapati + eggs/paneer + vegetables

That is a very different way of looking at food. You aren't taking food away. You're making the meal more complete.

And please don't skip dinner because lunch was heavy

I've seen women do this so many times. Lunch was bigger than planned. So dinner becomes: “I'm just going to have something very light.”

And then 9 pm comes. You're hungry. You're looking for something. And suddenly the mindful eating you've been trying so hard to practise goes out the window.

Your dinner doesn't have to be “diet food.” A balanced dinner with protein, vegetables and an appropriate portion of carbohydrate can absolutely fit into your day.

What are some easy Indian protein sources?

You don't need to live on chicken breast and protein shakes. You have plenty of options.

Vegetarian

Curd / Greek yogurt · Paneer · Tofu · Soy foods · Sambar or dal · Chana · Beans · Milk / soy milk

Non-vegetarian

Eggs · Fish · Chicken

The important thing is how much you're actually eating, not simply ticking a box saying “I had dal today.” A small amount of sambar or dal and a substantial serving are not the same thing. And protein content varies depending on the food, portion and preparation.

What if you're vegetarian?

You absolutely can get enough protein from a vegetarian diet. But you do need to be intentional.

A meal of rice + vegetables + a small amount of sambar or dal may not give you as much protein as you think.

So look for opportunities to add:

  • Curd
  • Greek yogurt
  • Paneer
  • Tofu
  • Soy
  • Lentils
  • Chana
  • Beans

And build them into the meals you're already eating.

Do you need protein powder?

No. Protein powder is not compulsory.

But it can be convenient. If you're working, travelling, rushing between responsibilities or simply struggling to get enough protein from food, a protein supplement can sometimes make things easier.

But I don't want you to think: “I need to buy protein powder first.” First look at your meals.

Can breakfast improve? Can lunch have a more substantial protein source? Can dinner have enough protein?

If you can improve those things, wonderful. If you're still struggling to reach your needs, then a protein supplement can be one practical option.

But here's what I don't want you to do

Please don't go from: “I need to eat less.” to “I need to eat huge amounts of protein.”

Protein is one piece of the picture.

  • Strength training
  • Vegetables and fibre
  • Everyday movement
  • Sleep
  • Stress management
  • Appropriate portions
  • A way of eating you can actually sustain

Because eating 120 grams of protein for three days and then giving up isn't the goal. Consistency matters more.

Try this today

Don't change everything tomorrow. Just look at what you ate yesterday.

Breakfast: __________

Lunch: __________

Dinner: __________

Now ask yourself:

  • Where was my protein at breakfast?
  • Where was it at lunch?
  • Where was it at dinner?
  • Was I getting most of it in just one meal?
  • Which one meal would be easiest for me to improve?

That's where I would start. Not everything. Just one meal.

And don't forget strength training

If you're increasing protein because you want to support your muscle, don't look at nutrition in isolation.

Protein provides the building blocks. Strength training gives your body the stimulus to maintain and build muscle.

That's why when I coach women 40+, I don't separate nutrition from movement.

How I approach protein with women 40+

At Eat Well Live Well, I don't want women spending the next decade moving from one restrictive diet to another.

I want you to understand your food. Understand your hunger. Understand how to build a satisfying Indian meal. Understand where protein fits. Understand the role of strength, sleep and movement.

And most importantly, build habits that fit your actual life.

Because your food should support your life. Not become another thing you have to constantly worry about.
