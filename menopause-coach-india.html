Why this stage can feel different

“I’m doing what I used to do. Why isn’t it working the same way?”

That is something many women ask as they move through their 40s and 50s.

Perimenopause and menopause happen alongside changes in hormones, ageing, muscle mass, sleep, recovery, stress and often a very demanding stage of life. That does not mean every symptom or every kilogram is caused by menopause. But it does mean that simply eating less and pushing harder is not always the most useful response.

This is where I want women to move away from punishment and towards a more thoughtful strategy.

Instead of asking only…

“How can I eat less?”

“Should I cut carbs?”

“Do I need more cardio?”

“Why don't I have enough willpower?”

We start asking…

Am I eating enough protein and fibre? Am I preserving muscle? How is my sleep? What drives my cravings? What can I repeat consistently in my real life?

What we work on

Five areas I keep coming back to with women in midlife

1. Nourish, don’t just restrict

Rather than starting with a longer list of foods to avoid, we look at the structure of your meals: protein, vegetables, fibre, carbohydrates, fats, portions, hunger and satisfaction.

2. Protect strength and muscle

Strength training becomes increasingly valuable in midlife. The goal is not simply to burn calories, but to build and preserve strength, function and confidence.

3. Take sleep seriously

Broken or insufficient sleep can make healthy choices feel much harder. We work on realistic routines and recovery rather than treating sleep as an afterthought.

4. Understand cravings & stress

Evening eating, emotional eating and cravings are not automatically a lack of discipline. We look at what is happening earlier in the day and what patterns keep repeating.

5. Build for consistency

A plan that works only on perfect weeks is not a sustainable plan. Work, family, travel, eating out and celebrations have to fit into the picture.

And keep it practical

You need to know what to do on an ordinary Tuesday — not only understand the theory. Coaching turns the big picture into small actions you can practise.

An Indian approach

You do not need a completely different “diet” from your family.

Rice, roti, idli, dosa, dal, curd, paneer, eggs, fish, vegetables and the foods you grew up with can all have a place.

We may change how a meal is put together: where the protein comes from, how much vegetable and fibre is present, the portion that works for you, how hungry you are when you begin eating, and what happens across the rest of your day.

The objective is not to make Indian food the problem. It is to help you understand how to make your normal food work better for your goals.

A few important questions

About menopause coaching

Is menopause coaching the same as medical menopause care?

No. Lifestyle coaching and medical care have different roles. Sushma coaches around nutrition, movement, sleep, stress, habits and behaviour change. She does not diagnose conditions, prescribe medication or replace a doctor. Symptoms that need medical assessment should be taken to an appropriate healthcare professional.

Can I work on weight loss during perimenopause or menopause?

Weight management can absolutely be one of your goals. The coaching approach looks beyond simply eating less and includes meal quality and structure, activity, strength, sleep, stress, hunger, cravings and consistency. Individual outcomes vary.

Will I have to give up rice, dosa or carbohydrates?

No. Coaching is not built around banning normal Indian foods. We look at the overall meal and eating pattern and make adjustments appropriate to your goals and preferences.

What if I already know what I “should” be doing?

That is very common. Knowing and consistently doing are different problems. Coaching helps identify what is getting in the way, choose a manageable focus and build behaviours you can repeat.

What if my sleep, periods or other symptoms have changed significantly?

Coaching can support healthy lifestyle behaviours, but new, severe or concerning symptoms should be discussed with your doctor. Coaching is designed to work alongside appropriate healthcare, not instead of it.
